# govspends

**Public-records investigations of government budgets, built in the open.**

Each investigation answers plain questions about a government's budget (is it higher, why is there a shortfall, where does the money go, who got a raise) from the government's own documents, and shows its work: every figure is traceable to a saved source document and page, the data tables are parsed by scripts anyone can re-run, and an audit summary explains how the report was made and what was corrected along the way. The pages here are the report itself. If you find an error or can add evidence, [propose a change](contributing.md); accepted changes update the website and its PDF.

## What you will find in each investigation

- **The report**, as web pages, with the same sections, tables and charts as the PDF (a PDF is rebuilt from these pages on every change).
- **References**: the URL, retrieval date and use of every document.
- **Sources and files**: every saved document, data file and script with its size and checksum, downloadable from the repository.
- **An audit summary**: how the evidence was gathered and processed, which figures are copied and which are computed, the judgment calls, the defects found and fixed, and how to re-verify.
- **Reviews**: independent reviews are published unchanged alongside the report, with the changes they led to.

## Provenance and standards

The first investigation was prepared with an AI assistant working from public records under a person's direction, then independently reviewed; that history is documented, not hidden. Facts come from primary documents where one exists; news reports are used for events, votes and quotations and are cited by URL. Nothing here is legal, financial or investment advice, and govspends is not affiliated with any government.
